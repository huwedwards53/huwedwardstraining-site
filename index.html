Impact in practice

From a training course to a Westminster Hall debate

One researcher's route from a policy engagement course to research cited in Parliament.

The researcher

A Senior Lecturer in Psychology at Manchester Metropolitan University, researching chronic pain in long‑term health conditions such as endometriosis.

Each step built on the one before: training gave her the map, coaching turned it into a plan, and a clear briefing gave her MP something to act on.

  1. Attended a Research Impact and Policy Engagement course.Course
  2. A one-to-one coaching session with Huw, and an action plan.Coaching
  3. Prepared briefing papers on her research with her university.University
  4. Contacted her MP, and spoke at a Westminster reception.Westminster
  5. Her MP tabled Parliamentary Questions on endometriosis.Commons
  6. Her work was cited in an Early Day Motion on endometriosis care.Commons
  7. Gave evidence to a Women and Equalities Select Committee inquiry, and was mentioned in its report.Committee
  8. Her MP referred to her work in a Westminster Hall debate on endometriosis, April 2026.Westminster Hall

The course

Research Impact and Policy Engagement

Training that supports university researchers in promoting their work to Parliament and policymakers, from engaging with MPs and Select Committees to briefing civil servants and Ministers.

The approach

  1. Every research subject has a policy focus.
  2. Engaging with Parliament and policymakers is essential, despite the difficulties.
  3. Parliament and government departments are a rich source of primary research material.
  4. Training and coaching in policy engagement can support all researchers, in all subjects, in all universities.

Parliament

Where the scrutiny happens

  • How Government Bills pass through the Commons and the Lords
  • Oral and written Parliamentary Questions, and how to draft them
  • Select Committee inquiries: written evidence, giving evidence as a witness, and advising a committee
  • Early Day Motions and All-Party Parliamentary Groups
  • House of Lords select committees and special inquiries
  • Hansard, the Commons and Lords Libraries, POST and the Knowledge Exchange Unit

Government

Where policy is made

  • Identifying the key departments, Ministers and public bodies for your work
  • How civil servants advise and Ministers decide
  • Responding to government consultations
  • Working with departmental Chief Scientific Advisers
  • Areas of Research Interest: where government is asking for research
  • Advisory groups, scientific advisory committees and working in the civil service

Briefing MPs

What do you want me to do?

  • What an MP looks for: constituency, political and campaigning interests
  • A two-page briefing that leads with the killer facts
  • A clear answer to the question every MP asks: what do you want me to do?
  • The MP's toolkit: letters, meetings with Ministers, questions, motions and debates
  • Print, broadcast and social media

Devolution

Four nations, many routes

  • The parliaments and governments of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
  • What is and is not devolved
  • Metro Mayors and Mayoral Combined Authorities in England

One-to-one coaching

Coaching for researchers whose work is ripe for policy engagement

Courses last half a day. In the other half of the day, Huw can meet individual researchers one to one.

How coaching works

  1. Meet Huw in person or online.
  2. Identify your research subject on the parliamentary record.
  3. Prepare a policy engagement action plan to promote your research.

Enquire about a course

Who are the courses for?

University researchers. Courses can be delivered for groups of Early Career and Senior Researchers and across academic disciplines, or tailored for specialist research institutes.

Where are courses held?

At any university. Courses last half a day, and Huw can meet individual researchers for one-to-one coaching in the other half of the day.

What will participants learn?

Practical routes into Parliament and government: drafting Parliamentary Questions and Early Day Motions, submitting evidence to Select Committees, writing the two-page briefing an MP wants, responding to government consultations, working with Chief Scientific Advisers and Areas of Research Interest, and how devolution shapes who to approach.

Does the training support the REF?

Yes. Huw supports universities as they prepare for the next REF, through training on research impact and policy engagement for their researchers.

Who is Huw Edwards?

Huw Edwards MBE is a university policy engagement trainer. He was MP for Monmouth and a university Senior Lecturer in Social Policy, and is currently Practitioner Researcher at the University of Wales, Lampeter, researching the parliamentary debate on Welsh devolution.
